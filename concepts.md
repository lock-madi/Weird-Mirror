# Idea Bank

## 1. Meditative Visual

* **Input:** Breathing, detected through the camera or another sensor. Deeper/slower breathing changes the visual.
* **Output:** A calm, generative environment that expands and contracts with your breath.
* **What changes:** The environment grows, flows, or becomes more expansive as you breathe deeply, then settles as you exhale.
* **How the viewer knows:** The visual follows their breathing rhythm, encouraging them to slow down and breathe more deeply.

## 2. Heartbeat

* **Input:** Live heart rate, potentially using an Apple Watch.
* **Output:** A visual that pulses or grows in time with the user's heartbeat.
* **What changes:** The speed and intensity of the visual changes with BPM. Something could flow, grow, or expand with every beat.
* **How the viewer knows:** The visual pulses at the same rhythm as their heartbeat, making their body directly visible on screen.

## 3. Music Reflection

* **Input:** Music played by the user.
* **Output:** A live reflection or image of the user that responds to the music.
* **What changes:** Colors, pixels, distortion, and movement change based on the music. Different genres could create different visual styles.
* **How the viewer knows:** The visual changes immediately when the music changes, making the connection between sound and image clear.

## 4. Conductor

* **Input:** Arm movements, including speed, direction, and rhythm.
* **Output:** A digital scene that builds or moves in response to the user's conducting.
* **What changes:** Slow movements could create smooth, complete visuals, while fast or irregular movements make the scene become choppy or chaotic.
* **How the viewer knows:** The scene directly follows the rhythm and speed of their movements, making the user feel like they are conducting the environment.

## 5. Interactive Dentist Waiting Room

* **Input:** Children's movement and interaction in front of a screen.
* **Output:** An interactive game or installation that teaches children how to take care of their teeth.
* **What changes:** The environment responds to the child's actions while introducing brushing, flossing, and dental hygiene concepts.
* **How the viewer knows:** The child can immediately see how their actions affect the characters or environment, while parents can observe from nearby without needing to actively entertain them.

## 6. Concert Visuals

* **Input:** Live music or a selected song.
* **Output:** Generative visuals designed specifically around an artist, album, or concert environment.
* **What changes:** Colors, movement, patterns, and imagery respond to the music and change with different parts of the song.
* **How the viewer knows:** The visuals move and change alongside the music, creating a visual extension of the artist's sound and aesthetic.

## 7. Alter Ego

* **Input:** The user's movements and appearance detected through the camera.
* **Output:** A digital reflection that initially behaves like a normal mirror.
* **What changes:** As the user interacts with it, the reflection begins to lag, distort, or move independently until it develops its own behavior.
* **How the viewer knows:** The reflection starts by copying them exactly, making the moment when it begins behaving differently immediately noticeable.

## 8. Tide

* **Input:** Hand position and movement. Raising or lowering the hands controls the height of the water, while faster movements create stronger waves.
* **Output:** A small ocean environment with a sailboat floating on the water.
* **What changes:** The tide rises and falls with the user's hands, while the boat rocks and moves with the waves. Facial expressions could also control the weather, with an unhappy expression creating rain and a smile bringing out the sun.
* **How the viewer knows:** The water responds directly to their hands, and the boat visibly reacts to the changing tide and waves.

## 9. Lighthouse

* **Input:** Oscillating arm movements.
* **Output:** The user becomes a lighthouse, with their arm movement controlling a sweeping beam of light across a nighttime environment.
* **What changes:** The direction and speed of the light changes with the user's movements, revealing different parts of the scene.
* **How the viewer knows:** Moving their arms causes the beam to sweep across the screen, revealing towns, ships, whales, or other objects hidden in the darkness.

## 10. Museum Statue

* **Input:** Body movement and stillness.
* **Output:** The user appears as a statue inside a digital art museum while particles gradually collect on their body.
* **What changes:** Standing still causes dust to accumulate quickly, while sudden movements scatter the particles.
* **How the viewer knows:** The longer they remain still, the more dust visibly builds up. Moving immediately sends the particles flying away.

## 11. Mother Nature

* **Input:** Hand position and an open-palm gesture.
* **Output:** A desolate landscape filled with dead or dying plants.
* **What changes:** Pouring water from the palm causes rain to fall over the plants, bringing them back to life. As the user moves to another part of the landscape, the plants they left behind begin to die again.
* **How the viewer knows:** The plants visibly respond to where the user chooses to direct the rain, creating a cycle of growth and decay.

## 12. Protect the Flame

* **Input:** One hand holds a virtual flame while the other hand protects it from wind and other elements.
* **Output:** A small flame surrounded by a dark environment, inspired by the atmosphere of *The Little Match Girl*.
* **What changes:** The flame grows stronger when protected and gradually builds over time. Wind, rain, or other environmental elements weaken it. Bringing the protecting hand too close can also extinguish the flame.
* **How the viewer knows:** The flame visibly flickers, grows, or shrinks based on the position of the user's hands, making the goal of protecting it immediately understandable.
